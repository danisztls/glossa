# Depositum — development plan

A living document: what's done, what's in flight, what's next, and why — organized by phase with dependencies and honest sizing. Distinct from `docs/decisions.md`, which is an **append-only historical log** of decisions as they were made (read it for _why_ something is the way it is, and when it changed). This document is the opposite shape: it gets rewritten as reality changes, has no history of its own, and exists to answer "what should someone work on next, and in what order." When a phase here ships, its "why" belongs in a new `decisions.md` entry, not a rewrite of this file's prose.

Sizing is stated honestly throughout: a measured figure is presented as one (with how it was measured); anything else is explicitly labelled an estimate. Where sizing isn't yet knowable (an item nobody has scoped in detail), that's stated too, rather than guessed at.

## Where things stand

### Done

- **Bible**: CPDV (English) and Matos Soares 1956 (Portuguese), 73/73 books each, both complete and validated.
- **Catechism of the Catholic Church**: `ccc.en` / `ccc.pt`, 2865/2865 paragraphs each.
- **Compendium of the CCC**: `compendium.en` / `compendium.pt`, 598/598 questions each.
- **Vatican II** (the first slice of what the user now calls the **Magisterium** group — conciliar, encyclical, exhortation, and CDF/DDF documents collectively; PT **Magistério**. `document` stays the schema-level work type, `/documents` the route — see `docs/corpus-schema.md` §Documents): all 16 constitutions/decrees/declarations, EN+PT, validated, cross-language symmetric (`pipeline/scrapers/vatican_docs.py`'s `check_language_symmetry`), with 4 documented, non-fixable source defects recorded in `docs/research/vatican-documents.md` §6.
- **Site**: Bible/Catechism/Compendium reading sections; language-coupled default editions with a per-work-type override (`docs/decisions.md`, "Content language follows UI language," 2026-08-15 — see this plan's Architecture section below for how Latin complicates it); Auto/light/dark/sepia themes; adjustable reading size; Lucide icons; universal reference linking (CCC footnote citations, `related`, in-prose refs, Compendium `ccc_refs`); Vulgate-canonical versification with Hebrew→Vulgate conversion on the way in; corpus split out of the eager JS bundle into immutable per-work `fetch()`-loaded files (`site/README.md`); an offline-first service worker with a two-tier cache (content tier precached indefinitely, shell tier versioned — `docs/decisions.md`'s "Offline caching implementation," 2026-08-15).

### In flight right now

- **Documents UI**: routes for the 32 Vatican II works (16 documents × 2 languages) that already exist in the corpus but aren't surfaced by any route yet (`site/README.md`: "not yet surfaced by any route, so their manifests ride along inertly in the index tier").
- **Encyclical sweep**: the Magisterium corpus's next slice. 216 documents discovered so far, crawl actively running against the phased list `docs/research/vatican-documents.md` §5 lays out (Phase 2's ~15–20 CCC-cited titles first, then the broader Phase 3 set).

## Sequencing and recommendation

The organizing question is **what unlocks what**. Four dependency threads matter more than the raw gap list above suggests:

1. **The reference system needs documents to point at.** `link-surface.md` inventory row #12 (`xrefs/ccc-documents.json`, resolving citations like "LG 12" to an actual link) is explicitly gated on documents being ingested — the CCC/encyclical citation _text_ is already captured verbatim in every `citations[]` array, waiting for something to resolve against. Vatican II is done; the encyclical sweep (in flight) is the rest of what this needs. Once both are far enough along and the documents UI (in flight) makes them navigable, building the `ccc-documents.json` xrefs pass is the highest-leverage single item on this list — it's the thing that makes the phrase "LG 12" clickable everywhere it already appears in already-scraped CCC text, without re-scraping anything.
2. **Latin multiplies every work's editions, so it should land after the N-editions-per-work-type mechanics are proven, not before.** Today every work type has at most 2 editions (en/pt). The site's data layer (`listEditions`, `defaultWorkId`) is already generic over however many editions exist — but the _reader-facing_ mechanics of picking one (the override, its persistence rules, the UI-language coupling) were designed and just-yesterday tuned for exactly 2, both of which are also UI languages. Landing Latin now would mean debugging that interaction for the first time on the highest-visibility possible change, at the same time as the Magisterium corpus is mid-ingestion. See Architecture, below, for the problem in full; Latin is placed in this plan as a **future, documented phase** — `docs/research/latin-sources.md` establishes what's fetchable, this plan does not schedule the fetch.
3. **The copyright posture has a hole that should close independent of feature work.** The colophon (#5) is small, has zero dependencies, and is the one item on this list whose absence is a standing risk to the project's entire stated posture (`docs/research/copyright.md` §5's "colophon states the position openly" is not a nice-to-have, it's part of what makes hosting without prior permission a _coherent_ position rather than just an omission). Same logic, one notch down, for per-work unpublish (#8): cheap relative to its risk-reduction value, and worth having _before_ the Magisterium corpus's document count outgrows the point where "we could take one down easily" is still credibly true.
4. **Search and the design pass are genuinely independent of all of the above** — and of each other. Neither blocks nor is blocked by documents, xrefs, or Latin. They compete for attention on priority, not sequencing.

**Recommended order:**

1. **Finish what's in flight** — documents UI, encyclical sweep — since both are already committed and everything in thread (1) above is gated on them.
2. **Colophon** (#5) — small, zero-dependency, closes a real posture gap; should not wait on anything else.
3. **Reverse cross-links** (#2) — data already exists, this is nearly-free relative to its value as the missing half of the stated flagship feature.
4. **`ccc-documents.json` xrefs + rendering** (part of thread 1 above, not separately numbered in the gap table since `link-surface.md` already scopes it) — once the encyclical sweep has enough of the CCC's top-cited titles landed, this is the next highest-leverage item: it's what turns the now-large Magisterium corpus into a linked one instead of a merely-hosted one.
5. **Per-work unpublish/degrade** (#8) and **per-work offline UI** (#6) — both cheap, both close real gaps between what's built (the mechanism/hook) and what's usable (no UI), worth doing together since they're the same shape of task.
6. **Search (#3), parallel view (#4), footnotes-as-sidenotes (#7), design pass (#9)** — run these on their own priority; none blocks or is blocked by the rest of this list. The design pass and footnotes-as-sidenotes share enough surface area (typography, spacing) that doing them together is likely more efficient than sequencing them, but that's a scheduling convenience, not a dependency.
7. **Enrichment** (#10, #11) — lowest urgency: both need an external, non-vatican.va source that hasn't been evaluated yet, so the first real step for either is a research pass in `docs/research/` style, not implementation.
8. **Latin** — scoped as a phase (below), not scheduled ahead of the above. Revisit once thread (1) has shipped and the Architecture question below has an actual decision recorded in `docs/decisions.md`.

## Latin — planned phase, not scheduled work

`docs/research/latin-sources.md` establishes, with live 2026-08-15 fetches, that Latin is fetchable and addressable for the Bible (two independent options — Nova Vulgata on vatican.va with Hebrew Psalm numbering, or the Clementine/Hetzenauer Vulgate from `sacredbible.org` matching the corpus's existing Vulgate numbering), all 16 Vatican II documents, the CCC, and a real (if uneven) slice of encyclicals — notably filling exactly the gap where Portuguese is weakest (Leo XIII: 64% Latin vs. 17% Portuguese, because Latin was the actual promulgation language). The Compendium has no Latin edition anywhere on vatican.va — a permanent gap, not a future scrape target.

This phase is **deliberately not scoped for implementation** here. What needs to happen before it can be:

- The Architecture question below needs an actual decision (a `docs/decisions.md` entry choosing one of the options, not just naming them).
- A choice between Nova Vulgata and Clementine/Hetzenauer for the Bible (or both, as separate editions — the schema already supports more than one edition per language) needs making, including the Psalm-numbering handling `latin-sources.md` §1 flags.
- The two-URL-template reality (`_lt.html` suffix for the "archive" template families vs. `/la/` path segment for the "content" template families, `latin-sources.md` §4) needs a scraper design that isn't assumed here.
- Ideally, thread (1) above (documents + xrefs) has shipped, so Latin editions of already-linked works are additive rather than landing into a still-being-built reference system.

Open questions for whoever scopes this next, not answered here: does Latin get its own corpus-wide `abbreviations.json` merge given the Latin CCC's populated `abbrev_lt.htm` (`latin-sources.md` §5)? Does the Magisterium family's own "Latin is normative, vernacular is translation" relationship (§3–4 of the research doc) change how `check_language_symmetry` should treat a Latin/vernacular mismatch versus an EN/PT one? Both are flagged, neither is decided.

## Architecture: UI language vs. content language, once Latin exists

**The problem, precisely.** `docs/decisions.md`'s "Content language follows UI language" (2026-08-15) made UI language the primary control: switching it switches both chrome and, by default, content edition, with a per-work-type override as the deliberate escape hatch for a reader who wants a specific edition regardless of interface language. That design is sound for a world where content languages and UI languages are the same two-element set (`en`/`pt`) — which is what `site/src/lib/i18n.svelte.ts`'s `UiLang = 'en' | 'pt'` type encodes, and what `content.svelte.ts`'s `ContentStore` was built against.

Latin breaks the premise, not the mechanism: **nobody is asking for a Latin interface, but readers will very much want the Latin text.** So for the population that wants Latin, the override isn't an escape hatch from a sensible default anymore — it's the _only_ path to what they want, since no UI language will ever default to Latin. Concretely, in the code as it stands today:

- `UiLang` (`i18n.svelte.ts`) is a closed two-value union. Latin can never be a `UiLang` value under the current design (correctly — nobody wants Latin chrome) — but that also means `content.langFor(type)`'s fallback (`?? i18n.lang`) and `defaultWorkId(type, lang)`'s matching logic (`corpus.ts`) will never _produce_ Latin as a default for any UI language, which is fine and intended.
- The problem is `content.svelte.ts`'s override lifecycle: `ContentStore#set` stores `{ workId, forUiLang: i18n.lang }`, and `#activeOverride` discards the override the moment `i18n.lang` no longer matches `forUiLang`. This is exactly the right behavior for the case the Aug-15 decision was designed around (a reader who picked the PT Bible under an EN interface probably _does_ want that reset when they deliberately switch interface language — that's the decision's own stated rationale). It is exactly the _wrong_ behavior for a reader who picks the Latin Bible: there is no UI-language event that should ever "naturally" supersede a Latin pick, because Latin was never reachable as anyone's default in the first place. As built today, a Latin override would silently evaporate the next time the reader touches the language switcher for any reason — an unpleasant surprise for exactly the readers this feature exists to serve.
- `listEditions(type)` and `defaultWorkId(type, lang)` (`corpus.ts`) are, encouragingly, already language-agnostic — they sort/filter over `WorkManifest.language` generically and don't hardcode `en`/`pt` anywhere. Adding a Latin `WorkManifest` needs no change here. The friction is entirely in the override's lifecycle and in `UiLang`'s type, not in the corpus access layer.
- `pipeline/scrapers/vatican_docs.py`'s `check_language_symmetry` hardcodes a two-language world at both ends: `_WORK_ID_RE = re.compile(r"^([a-z][a-z-]*)\.(.+)\.(en|pt)$")` only recognizes `en`/`pt` as valid trailing language codes at all (a Latin work directory wouldn't even match the regex, so it would be silently excluded from validation rather than checked), and the pairwise comparison (`"en" not in langs or "pt" not in langs: continue`) is written for exactly two languages, not N.
- `docs/decisions.md`'s "Language symmetry principle" (2026-08-14) describes the mechanism in explicitly binary terms — "the toggle keeps the ID, swaps the language" — language that assumes exactly two co-equal translations. It stops fitting once a third language exists for only some works (Latin exists for the Bible, Vatican II, CCC, many encyclicals — never for the Compendium), and once one of those languages (Latin, for conciliar/papal texts specifically — `latin-sources.md` §3–4) is not a co-equal translation at all but the **normative text the others translate**.

**Options.**

1. **Split `ContentLang` from `UiLang` as a type, and make the override's persistence conditional on whether its language is itself a UI language.** `UiLang` stays `'en' | 'pt'` — the interface never needs more. A separate, open `ContentLang` (or just `string`, since `corpus.ts` already treats language generically) is used wherever an edition's _language_ is discussed, distinct from the interface's language. The one behavioral change needed: `ContentStore`'s reset-on-UI-switch rule should fire only when the override's content language is also a UI language (today's `en`/`pt` case, unchanged) — a pick whose language _isn't_ a UI language (Latin) has no UI event that should ever clear it, so it persists until the reader explicitly changes it. This is a small, precisely scoped change to one function's condition, not a rewrite of the store.
2. **Make every override sticky regardless of language — drop `forUiLang` scoping entirely.** Simpler mental model (one rule: an override persists until changed, full stop), but it re-opens the specific problem the Aug-15 decision solved for the `en`/`pt` case: a reader who picks the PT Bible under an EN interface, then deliberately switches to a PT interface expecting PT content throughout, keeps a Bible edition that's now redundant with the new default rather than resetting to it. Trades away a one-day-old decision's stated benefit ("fewer surprising states") for the `en`/`pt` majority, to simplify handling the Latin minority.
3. **Fully decouple UI language and content language for every work, as two independent, always-explicit controls** — closest to the _original_ (pre-Aug-15) design this project already tried and reversed once. Most conceptually honest given Latin's existence, but solves a problem Latin doesn't actually create for `en`/`pt` readers, and re-opens a decision made specifically to reduce surprising states for the common case.

**Recommendation: Option 1.** It's the narrowest change that preserves the Aug-15 decision's benefit for the population it was designed for (`en`/`pt` readers, the overwhelming majority) while making the override genuinely load-bearing for the population Latin actually affects, without re-litigating a one-day-old decision or over-generalizing past what's actually needed. The corpus/data layer already supports it with no changes (`listEditions`/`defaultWorkId` are already generic); the work is confined to `UiLang`'s type declaration and one conditional in `ContentStore`.

**For the pipeline check**, the minimum fix is mechanical — generalize `_WORK_ID_RE` to accept any language code, and generalize the pairwise comparison to run over however many languages are actually present for a given `(family, slug)`, not just `en`/`pt`. The more interesting, undecided question is _what_ the check should assert once Latin is one of the languages compared: `check_language_symmetry` currently treats a section-count mismatch as evidence of a parsing defect, reasonable when comparing two independent translations of the same source. Once Latin (the normative text for conciliar/papal documents) is in the comparison set, a Latin/vernacular mismatch might be a real translation-structure difference rather than a defect — comparing each vernacular _against Latin specifically_, when Latin is present, may be the more principled check than comparing all present languages pairwise against each other. Not resolved here; a genuine design question for whoever builds Latin ingestion, flagged so it isn't rediscovered mid-implementation the way `check_language_symmetry` itself was originally motivated by a real, found defect (`docs/research/vatican-documents.md` §6).

**`docs/decisions.md`'s "toggle" language** should be revisited in a future dated entry once an option above is actually chosen — not rewritten here, since this plan doesn't alter the historical log.
