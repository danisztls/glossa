# Latin as a source language

Research conducted 2026-08-15 (Claude, read-only survey; live `curl` fetches against `www.vatican.va` and `sacredbible.org`, both network-allowlisted for this task). Companion to `bible-texts.md`, `vatican-documents.md`, and `copyright.md`. Written to establish, empirically, whether Latin is actually fetchable and addressable before it's scheduled as a source language — see `../../PLAN.md`'s Latin phase for how these findings translate into scoped work, and `../decisions.md`'s 2026-08-15 "Latin as next source language" entry for the decision this feeds.

**Scope note**: this document only establishes availability and addressability. Nothing here is scraped, parsed, or ingested — `pipeline/` is untouched by this task. See `../../PLAN.md` for sequencing.

## TL;DR

Latin is present on vatican.va for every family we currently ingest **except the Compendium**, which has no Latin edition anywhere on the site — confirmed absent, not merely unlinked. The Nova Vulgata (the Church's own modern critical Latin Bible) is fully addressable, book-by-book, with the same "inline plain-digit" verse convention the existing scrapers already parse defensively — but its Psalter uses **Hebrew/Masoretic numbering**, not the Vulgate/Septuagint numbering both v1 Bible editions and the whole corpus's versification system assume, which is a real, non-cosmetic wrinkle. The Clementine Vulgate isn't a vatican.va text at all — it lives at `sacredbible.org`, the same site and operator as our existing CPDV source, in an edition (Hetzenauer 1914) that is in fact the exact Latin base CPDV was translated from, explicitly disclaimed as public domain, and — unlike the Nova Vulgata — uses the traditional Vulgate psalm numbering that already matches our schema. All 16 Vatican II documents have `_lt.html` editions, confirmed present and using the same numbering convention as their EN/PT siblings. Encyclical Latin coverage is uneven by pontificate but, notably, **much stronger for Leo XIII than Portuguese is** (64% vs. 17%) — because Latin was these documents' actual promulgation language, not a later translation. The CCC's Latin edition exists, is chapter-paginated the same way the EN mirror is, and — a genuine bonus — its front-matter abbreviations page is **populated**, unlike the EN/PT mirrors which are empty (`link-surface.md` #4).

## 1. Nova Vulgata

**Available.** Index: `https://www.vatican.va/archive/bible/nova_vulgata/documents/nova-vulgata_index_lt.html` (200, retrieved 2026-08-15). Structure: `nova-vulgata_praefatio_lt.html`, `nova-vulgata_praenotanda_lt.html` (front matter), `nova-vulgata_vetus-testamentum_lt.html` and `nova-vulgata_novum-testamentum_lt.html` (per-testament indexes), `nova-vulgata_appendix_lt.html`, `nova-vulgata_jp-ii_apost_const_lt.html` (the _Scripturarum Thesaurus_ apostolic constitution promulgating it).

The OT index lists all 46 books in the full Catholic canon including deuterocanonicals under their Latin names (`nova-vulgata_vt_thobis_lt.html` = Tobit, `nova-vulgata_vt_ecclesiasticus_lt.html` = Sirach, `nova-vulgata_vt_baruch_lt.html`, `nova-vulgata_vt_i-maccabaeorum_lt.html`/`ii-maccabaeorum`, `sapientiae` = Wisdom); the NT index lists all 27. One page per book (not per chapter) — same granularity as CPDV's sacredbible.org source, not the CCC's multi-page-per-part convention.

**Addressability, verified directly (Genesis, fetched 2026-08-15):** chapters are `<a name="N">` anchors; verses are plain leading digits followed by the verse text run together with `<br />` breaks, e.g. `<a name="1">1</a>... 1 In principio creavit Deus caelum et terram. 2 Terra autem erat inanis et vacua...`. No per-verse markup element — a verse boundary is "a digit, optionally with leading whitespace, immediately followed by a space and then text," recoverable by regex the same way CPDV's `{c:v}` markers or the encyclical family's inline paragraph numbers already are, just without the curly-brace delimiter CPDV happens to have. This is a new, mildly fiddlier micro-pattern, not a new parsing paradigm.

**The Psalter uses Hebrew/Masoretic numbering, not Vulgate/Septuagint numbering — confirmed, not inferred.** The psalms index lists `PSALMUS 1` through `PSALMUS 150` as 150 _distinct_ anchors, including a standalone `PSALMUS 10` immediately after `PSALMUS 9` — impossible under the traditional Vulgate convention, where 9 and 10 are one merged psalm. Fetching `PSALMUS 10` directly confirms it: the page prints `PSALMUS 10 (Vg 9, 22-39)` — an explicit parenthetical cross-reference telling the reader "this is old-Vulgate Psalm 9, verses 22–39." The Nova Vulgata's 1979 revision deliberately adopted the modern Hebrew enumeration as primary while keeping a parenthetical old-Vulgate pointer for cross-reference — a well-known fact about the edition, now verified against the live page rather than assumed.

This matters concretely: `corpus-schema.md`'s Bible manifest carries a single `psalm_numbering` field (`"vulgate"` — both v1 editions agree), and `link-surface.md`'s "Versification" section states plainly that Glossa Catholica's canonical Bible address space _is_ Vulgate numbering, with Hebrew numbering as something converted **on the way in** from citations/jump-box input, never stored. A Nova Vulgata edition inverts that relationship for the one work itself: its own native text is Hebrew-numbered. Ingesting it means either (a) adding `"psalm_numbering": "hebrew"` as a legitimate second manifest value and converting Nova Vulgata's numbers to Vulgate at parse time so the corpus stays internally uniform (consistent with the existing "Vulgate is canonical" principle, more parser work up front), or (b) storing it Hebrew-numbered as printed and converting at the versification layer same as citations already are (less parser work, but means the existing `psalm_numbering: "vulgate"` assumption threaded through `versification.ts` needs to become genuinely per-edition instead of corpus-wide). Not resolved here — a real decision for whoever scopes Latin Bible ingestion, flagged so it isn't discovered mid-implementation the way the Malachi/Joel gap was (`link-surface.md`'s own retrospective on that).

## 2. Clementine Vulgate

**Not a vatican.va publication — confirmed via the CCC citation apparatus's own consumption of it, and via direct site search.** No `nova-vulgata`-style Clementine mirror exists under `vatican.va/archive/bible/`; the only Latin Bible vatican.va hosts is the Nova Vulgata (§1).

**Found at `sacredbible.org`** — the same domain and operator (Ronald L. Conte Jr.) as the existing CPDV source (`bible-texts.md`'s ranked scrape sources, #1). The site hosts **four** Vulgate editions side by side, discoverable from its root index:

- `sacredbible.org/vulgate1822/` — Clementine, 1822 printing
- `sacredbible.org/vulgate1861/`
- `sacredbible.org/vulgate1914/` — **the Hetzenauer 1914 edition** — titled on-page "Latin Bible, Biblia Sacra, Vulgatae Editionis, Hetzenauer 1914" — this is the exact edition `bible-texts.md`'s CPDV assessment already names as CPDV's translation base ("verse-by-verse translation of the Clementine Vulgate (1914 Hetzenauer base)"). Using this specific edition would make Glossa Catholica's English (CPDV) and Latin editions a genuine matched translation pair, not just two unrelated Latin-family texts.
- `sacredbible.org/vulgate2009/`

**Rights status, checked directly (2026-08-15):** every page's `<meta name="copyright">` tag reads verbatim **"All text and images, including all html and css code, is in the public domain. No copyright."** This answers the brief's question about the transcription itself, not just the underlying 1592 text: Conte's site explicitly disclaims any transcription-layer claim, the same posture it takes for the CPDV pages we already scrape from the same domain. The underlying Hetzenauer 1914 apparatus (editor Michael Hetzenauer, Capuchin biblical scholar, d. 1927) is separately long past any life+70 window regardless. No open rights question here, unlike Matos Soares.

**Addressability, verified directly (John, Psalms):** identical `{c:v}` inline marker convention to CPDV — `{1:1}`, `{1:2}`, … — trivially reusable by a near-copy of the existing CPDV parser, same URL naming scheme (`NT-04_Joannes.htm`, `VT-21_Psalmi.htm`, one file per book).

**Psalm numbering is the traditional Vulgate/Septuagint convention, confirmed, and this is the key contrast with the Nova Vulgata (§1):** the 1914 edition's Psalms file shows `{9:20}` through `{9:29}` as part of a single Psalm 9 (no standalone `{10:1}...{10:9}` block before it) — the classic 9/10 merge. This edition slots into the existing corpus with `"psalm_numbering": "vulgate"` unchanged — no versification-layer rework needed, unlike Nova Vulgata.

**Net effect**: two very different Latin Bible options exist, not one. Clementine/Hetzenauer is the lower-friction ingestion (matches existing schema assumptions exactly, matches the existing CPDV parser shape, explicitly PD, thematically paired with CPDV) but is the _pre-conciliar_ text — not what a reader looking for "the Church's current official Latin Bible" would expect. Nova Vulgata is the Church's actual current liturgical/typical-edition Latin text (promulgated by John Paul II, used in the current Roman Missal and Liturgy of the Hours) but requires the versification decision above and a new page-shape micro-parser. Both are real options; nothing here picks one — see `../../PLAN.md`.

## 3. Vatican II documents in Latin

**All 16/16 confirmed present**, `_lt.html` editions linked directly from the same council index page `vatican-documents.md` §2 already used for its EN/PT audit (`https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/index.htm`, re-fetched 2026-08-15): all 4 constitutions, all 3 declarations, all 9 decrees. Sample-fetched three spanning the family's date range and document kinds — _Lumen Gentium_ (const., 1964), _Ad Gentes_ (decree, 1965), _Inter Mirifica_ (decree, 1963) — all 200.

**Numbering convention matches EN/PT, confirmed directly on Lumen Gentium**: paragraph 1 opens `1. Collegium episcoporum...`, the same bare "digit, period, space" convention `vatican-documents.md` §3 already validated for the EN mirror. No new parsing family needed for Vatican II Latin specifically — same parser, third `lang` argument.

**Why this matters more than a fourth language slot**, per this task's brief: for conciliar (and papal) documents, **Latin is the promulgated, normative text; English and Portuguese are translations of it.** This is not how the corpus currently frames any of its language pairs — `decisions.md`'s "Language symmetry principle" and `corpus-schema.md` both treat EN and PT as two co-equal editions of the same work, with no "original" language singled out. Vatican II Latin breaks that symmetry conceptually even before it breaks it technically (see `../../PLAN.md`'s architecture section): a documented, citable divergence between the Latin and a vernacular translation is not automatically a _defect_ in the vernacular the way an EN/PT paragraph-count mismatch currently is treated by `vatican_docs.py`'s `check_language_symmetry` — it might just be what translation is. Worth stating plainly since it's easy to reach for the existing "symmetry = correctness oracle" tooling and misapply it once a normative-vs-translation relationship exists rather than a translation-vs-translation one.

## 4. Encyclicals in Latin

Sampled the same four pontificates `vatican-documents.md` §2 already audited for EN/PT, using the equivalent Latin index (`https://www.vatican.va/content/{pontiff}/la/encyclicals.index.html`), counting distinct `.../encyclicals/documents/{slug}.html` links the same way that survey did:

| Pontificate  | Distinct encyclicals (from EN audit) | LA available    | EN available | PT available |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------ | --------------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| Leo XIII     | 86                                   | **55/86 (64%)** | 86/86 (100%) | 15/86 (17%)  |
| Pius XII     | 41                                   | **40/41 (98%)** | 40/41 (98%)  | 34/41 (83%)  |
| John Paul II | 14                                   | **13/14 (93%)** | 14/14 (100%) | 14/14 (100%) |
| Francis      | 4                                    | **4/4 (100%)**  | 4/4 (100%)   | 4/4 (100%)   |

(All counts retrieved 2026-08-15, same methodology as `vatican-documents.md` §2: fetch the pontiff's per-language `encyclicals.index.html`, extract distinct `documents/{slug}.html` hrefs, count.)

**The Leo XIII number is the interesting one, and it inverts the PT story.** Portuguese coverage for Leo XIII is famously bad (17%, `vatican-documents.md`/`decisions.md`) because vatican.va's Portuguese mirror is a later, partial back-translation effort. Latin coverage for the same pontificate is **64%** — nearly four times better — because for a document from 1891, Latin isn't a translation being retrofitted, it's _what the document was actually promulgated in_. This is a real, citable point in favor of prioritizing Latin ingestion for exactly the older-pontificate documents where PT is weakest: a reader who hits a PT-less Leo XIII encyclical today gets nothing in their non-English language; with Latin ingested, they'd have the actual authoritative text available even where no vernacular exists.

**URL scheme differs by document family/era, worth flagging for whoever writes the scraper**: Vatican II Latin (§3) uses the older "archive" template's `_lt.html` filename suffix; encyclical/exhortation/CDF Latin (this section, confirmed also for Dominus Iesus, `https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000806_dominus-iesus_lt.html`, 200) uses the newer "content" template's `/la/` path segment (`/content/{pontiff}/la/encyclicals/documents/{slug}.html`). This is the same two-template split `vatican-documents.md` §2's "site reliability note" already documents for EN/PT (pre-2016 "archive" frameset pages vs. post-2016 Dicastery-branded "content" pages) — Latin doesn't introduce a third template, it just means the scraper needs `lang` to route through whichever of the two URL shapes the family already uses, exactly as it already must for EN/PT.

**Not measured**: Latin coverage for the pontificates `vatican-documents.md` §2 flagged as inferred-not-measured for EN/PT (Pius XI, Pius X, Benedict XV, Paul VI, John XXIII, Benedict XVI, John Paul I) was not checked here either — out of scope for this survey, same honest-gap caveat applies.

## 5. CCC in Latin

**Present.** `https://www.vatican.va/archive/catechism_lt/index_lt.htm` (200, retrieved 2026-08-15) — `<meta name="language" content="la" />`, title "Catechismus Catholicae Ecclesiae - Index Generalis", `eventDate` 1992 (the year of the CCC's original promulgation; the Latin _editio typica_ itself dates to 1997, per _Laetamur Magnopere_, which made Latin the CCC's own definitive reference text — the same normative-vs-translation relationship noted for Vatican II in §3, applying here too).

**Paginated the same way as the EN mirror**, confirmed by fetching the index and a content page directly: `p1s1c1_lt.htm`, `p1s2c1p1_lt.htm`, `p2_lt.htm`, `p3_lt.htm`, `p4s1_lt.htm`, `prologue_lt.htm`, `lettera-apost_lt.htm`, `aposcons_lt.htm` — same `p{part}s{section}c{chapter}...` slug shape `ccc.py` already parses for EN. Paragraph numbering confirmed on `p1s1c1_lt.htm`: bold-tagged numbers (`<b>26</b>`, `<b>27</b>`, `<b>28</b>`…) in the same convention the existing scraper targets.

**Bonus finding, not asked for but worth recording since it touches a known gap**: `abbrev_lt.htm` (linked from the Latin index as "SIGLA") **is populated** — it carries `ABBREVIATIONES PRO SACRA SCRIPTURA` with separate `VETUS TESTAMENTUM` / `NOVUM TESTAMENTUM` sections, confirmed by fetching the page directly. This is the exact table `link-surface.md` #4 and `corpus-schema.md`'s `abbreviations.json` document as **empty in both current sources** ("neither vatican.va CCC mirror carries the front-matter table"). The Latin mirror does carry it. `link-surface.md` already notes this gap is being resolved a different way (each ingested Vatican II/encyclical document's own title becomes the decoder ring for its own siglum, once those documents exist) — so this isn't a blocker being unblocked, just a second, independent path to the same data that exists if the primary one is ever short.

## 6. Compendium in Latin

**Not present. Confirmed absent, not merely unlinked.** The Compendium's own document index (`https://www.vatican.va/archive/compendium_ccc/documents/archive_2005_compendium-ccc_en.html`, fetched 2026-08-15) lists every language edition it has as sibling links: `en`, `fr`, `ge`, `hu`, `it`, `po` (Portuguese), `ro`, `sl`, `sp`, `sv` as HTML, plus `be` (Belarusian), `id` (Indonesian), `ru` (Russian) as PDF-only. No `_lt.html`, no `_lt.pdf`, no Latin entry of any kind in that list. A direct guess at the predictable `_lt.html` filename (`archive_2005_compendium-ccc_lt.html`) independently returned 404, and `archive/compendium_ccc/index_lt.htm` also 404s. (One filename in the list, `compendium_catech_lit.pdf`, uses `lit` rather than `lt` — this is vatican.va's own disambiguation, not a hidden Latin file: `lt` is reserved sitewide for Latin, as confirmed everywhere else in this document, so language codes that would otherwise collide with it — Lithuanian here — get spelled out instead.)

**What this means, stated plainly**: the Compendium — alone among the four work types Glossa Catholica currently ingests — has **no Latin source to ingest, ever, from vatican.va.** Unlike the Bible (two independent Latin options, §1–2) or CCC/Vatican II/encyclicals (present, sometimes richly so), a Latin Compendium edition is not a scraping problem to be solved with more effort; it is a gap in what the Holy See itself published. If Latin ever becomes a real edition axis in the site (see `../../PLAN.md`), the Compendium is the one current work type where "Latin: not available" is a permanent, source-level fact to design around from day one — not a "phase 2" placeholder the way, say, a not-yet-scraped encyclical is.

## Honest gaps in this survey

- CDF/DDF Latin coverage beyond the single Dominus Iesus spot-check (§4) was not surveyed for breadth.
- Apostolic exhortations/constitutions were not separately checked for Latin (only encyclicals and the one CDF document); `vatican-documents.md` already treats exhortations as "same parser family, lower priority than the ranked encyclical list," and that framing likely extends to their Latin availability, but this is inferred, not measured.
- Pontificates between the four sampled here (§4) were not checked, same honest-gap caveat `vatican-documents.md` §2 already carries for EN/PT.
- No attempt was made to resolve which of Nova Vulgata vs. Clementine/Hetzenauer (or both) should actually be ingested — that is a scoping decision, not a research finding, and is left to `../../PLAN.md`.
