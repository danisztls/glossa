# The link surface

Glossa Catholica's value is dense internal linking. This document inventories **every reference apparatus in the source texts**, what the corpus captures for each, and why the deferred ones are safe to defer. Companion to `corpus-schema.md` (the how); this is the what and why.

## Principle

The corpus stores **positions and raw strings, never interpretations**. Citation strings stay verbatim, markers stay where printed, marginal numbers stay in printed order. Parsing, normalization, and link resolution happen in later derived passes — a better parser next year improves every link without touching scraped data. Insurance policy: every raw source page is cached in `corpus/raw/`, so any capture regret is fixable by **re-parsing, never re-crawling**.

## Why no Bible footnotes in v1

Two different reasons, one per edition:

- **CPDV: none exist.** Conte published the CPDV deliberately without annotations (his stated position: Canon 825 requires annotations _and_ approval, and he refused the approval process). The online edition has no notes to capture.
- **Matos Soares: notes exist, our source lacks them.** The translator's apologetic/explanatory footnotes are real, but liriocatolico chapter pages print only the markers (`[i]`) without the note content. The sole structured source carrying the notes is vulgata.online — Cloudflare-protected, self-described "EM FASE DE DESENVOLVIMENTO". Deferred as a future enrichment pass; recorded in the work's manifest. (The notes are also under the same copyright clock as the translation, PD 1 Jan 2028.)

The schema's per-verse `notes` field already exists, so adding footnotes later — Matos Soares via vulgata.online, or Douay-Rheims with its public-domain Challoner notes — is additive: no schema change, no re-architecture.

## Deferred with rationale

- **Inline emphasis (italics)** in CCC text: v1 loss, recoverable from `corpus/raw/`. Not a link surface, purely typographic.
- **CCC front matter** (_Fidei Depositum_): unnumbered, participates in no linking; becomes an ordinary document in v2.

## Versification: Vulgate is canonical, Hebrew/Masoretic is converted on the way in

Both v1 Bible editions print **Vulgate** chapter/verse numbering (`psalm_numbering: "vulgate"` in every Bible manifest) — this is Glossa Catholica's canonical Bible address space. An incoming reference may be phrased in **Hebrew/Masoretic** numbering instead — the convention most modern translations (NAB, RSV, NIV, …) use where it diverges from the Vulgate — and needs converting before it can resolve against the corpus.

This was originally scoped here as Psalms-only, jump-box-only ("both v1 editions use Vulgate numbering, so 'Psalm 23' opening Psalm 22 is jump-box logic, not corpus data"). That scoping was wrong on both axes, found by a real prerender failure (a dead link from `/ccc/678` to `/bible/cpdv.en/mal/3#v19` — Malachi 3 stops at verse 18 in the Vulgate; Hebrew 3:19 is Vulgate 4:1):

- **Not Psalms-only.** Malachi (Hebrew 3:19–24 = Vulgate 4:1–6) and Joel (Hebrew 3:1–5 = Vulgate 2:28–32; Hebrew 4 = Vulgate 3) both diverge and are both live in the real CCC citations. A corpus-wide measurement (every citation in both `ccc.en`/`ccc.pt`, checked against both real Bible editions) found the Psalms divergence itself is pervasive — most Psalm citations outside chapters 1–8 and 148–150 need it, not an edge case.
- **Not jump-box-only.** The CCC's own footnote citations are a second, independent consumer: `citations[].text` cites scripture in whichever numbering the source used, same as the jump box's typed input.

Where the conversion happens:

- **`site/scripts/build-xrefs.mjs`** — converts every parsed citation to Vulgate before writing the CCC → Bible index, so that index is already in the canonical address space. It calls `versification.ts`, same as the reader does. (Until 2026-08-21 this was `pipeline/build/versification.py` feeding `pipeline/build/xrefs.py`, which wrote a committed `corpus/xrefs/ccc-bible.json`. Both are deleted and `pipeline/build/` is gone with them; see `decisions.md` for why the index is derived at build time now.)
- **`site/src/lib/versification.ts`** — the TypeScript counterpart (same mapping tables, corpus-verified against both real Bible editions), used by `refs.ts`'s `refHref` (CCC footnote rendering) and `refparse.ts` (the jump box, so "Psalm 23" still opens Vulgate Psalm 22 — the original case this section named).

Both modules are pure and corpus-agnostic (data tables + an injected existence check), covering the divergences actually confirmed in the corpus (Psalms, Malachi, Joel) plus the well-known Psalm chapter merges/splits (9+10, 114+115, 116, 147). A handful of other apparent mismatches turned up by the same measurement (a few 2 Corinthians/Acts/Matthew verse-count quirks, and several plain transcription typos) are NOT Hebrew/Vulgate divergences — the Masoretic text only covers the Old Testament — and are deliberately left unmapped rather than guessed at; see `versification.ts`'s module docblock for the full accounting.

## v2 surfaces this design anticipates

- **Encyclicals & council documents**: scoped in 2026-08-15 (see `decisions.md` §Scope) — confirmed, not merely anticipated. The scoping survey (`research/vatican-documents.md`) found every document family tested — Vatican II's 16 constitutions/decrees/declarations, encyclicals across four pontificates (1891–2015), an apostolic exhortation, a CDF declaration — uses the same inline paragraph-numbering convention `ccc.py` already parses, and vatican.va serves all 16 Vatican II documents plus the CCC's ~30 most-cited encyclicals/exhortations in both languages (some Leo XIII-era ones landing English-only, an accepted exception — see `decisions.md`). Once ingested (schema: `corpus-schema.md` §Documents), the raw `citations` strings + `abbreviations.json` let the parser resolve "LG 12" to an actual paragraph link — bidirectionally (CCC ¶ ↔ LG §12) — exactly as originally anticipated here; see inventory row #12 for the derived xref pass.
- **Bible → Bible parallels** (synoptic cross-refs): none of our v1 sources carry them; would come from a separate PD apparatus, joined by OSIS address.
- **Denzinger**: confirmed out of scope for a vatican.va-sourced pipeline — Herder-copyrighted, never a vatican.va publication (`research/vatican-documents.md` §2), despite being the single most-cited non-scripture siglum in the CCC (502 occurrences).
- **The Summa is no longer future work** — ingested 2026-08-23 (`corpus-schema.md` §Summa, `decisions.md`). 248 citations across 55 works resolve against it; `refs.ts` links them at `/doctores/summa/{part}/{question}#a{n}` (`/summa/...` until the shelf for the Fathers and Doctors landed on 2026-08-28 — `decisions.md` §Addresses and editions), edition-free, with the reader's edition chosen by fallback rather than named in the URL. It also brings a link surface of its own in the other direction: 7,582 scripture references linkified out of the English edition's prose.
- **The Fathers are deliberately NOT ingested**, and this is a decision rather than a backlog item (`decisions.md` §Scope). They are not one work but a library: 1,244 citations across 157 distinct Migne volumes and ~288 abbreviated work-titles, with a 34% tail of minor authors, no Portuguese source at any price, and an English base (ANF/NPNF) that is an anthology rather than a complete corpus. What is recommended instead is a parse of the citation strings already captured — **94% carry a work-internal locator** (`Adv. haeres. 3, 20, 2`) quite apart from the Migne column — which buys reverse "cited in" panels and link-out targets for no fetches and no rights exposure. Full measurement in `research/summa-and-fathers.md`.
